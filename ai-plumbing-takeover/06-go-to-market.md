# 06 — Go-To-Market

The category has no incumbent. That's the good news and the hard news. With no incumbent, you also have no established buying pattern to exploit. We have to create the buyer.

## 1. The wedge

**Who:** Facilities managers at mid-size commercial real estate operators (50-500 buildings). Not the biggest REITs (too slow to buy). Not independent building owners (no budget discipline). The sweet spot is regional commercial property managers.

**What they care about (in order):**
1. Water damage claims (highest frequency insurance loss)
2. Tenant retention (a burst pipe that takes out three floors = tenants leave)
3. Insurance premiums (they see the renewal bill every year)
4. Maintenance budget predictability
5. Doing nothing different (they are VERY risk averse)

**Our pitch to them:** *"Zero dollars upfront. Zero changes to your plumber. We install sensors in one building. If we're wrong about the 72-hour prediction, you pay nothing. If we're right, you save an average $84,000 in prevented damage per building per year."*

That's the wedge. It has to be free to try and risk-free to adopt.

## 2. Launch metro

**Recommendation: Austin, TX** (alternative: Denver).

Why Austin:
- High insurance premiums (Texas freeze events are now underwriting catastrophes)
- Lots of mid-size commercial real estate with active maintenance budgets
- Weak plumber labor supply (strong need for efficiency tools)
- Active startup ecosystem for hiring
- Lower cost of operations than coastal metros
- Proximity to manufacturing (Monterrey, MX within 8 hours)
- No state income tax (founder / early team quality of life)

Why not:
- ~~San Francisco~~ — too expensive, too saturated with tech-first buyers, pipe infrastructure is mostly old SF buildings that are harder to instrument
- ~~New York~~ — union complexity, regulatory maze, 18-month sales cycles
- ~~Miami~~ — great for demand (hurricane water damage) but immature commercial RE operator layer

## 3. The 10 pilot playbook (Year 1)

### Month 1-2: Pre-install
- Hire head of trade partnerships (in Austin)
- Identify 30 target commercial property operators via public records + networking
- Land 10 paid pilots via founder-led sales (cold outreach + intros)
- Each pilot: 20-40 sensors, 1 building, $29/sensor/month, 12-month commit

### Month 3-4: Install
- Deploy 240 sensors total across 10 buildings
- Self-install by facilities teams (proving the install UX)
- Baseline data collection begins immediately

### Month 5-12: Prove the thesis
- Capture acoustic data 24/7 across all sensors
- Partner with 3-5 independent plumbers in Austin for ground-truth labeling (when a real failure happens, they're our labelers)
- Iterate the model weekly
- Track every alert, every outcome, every false positive
- Target: 94% precision, 89% recall by month 12

### Deliverable at end of Year 1
A written, auditable case study:
- "X critical failures predicted >72 hours in advance"
- "$Y in prevented damage across 10 buildings"
- "Z false positives (and how we fixed them)"

This case study becomes the sales asset for the Seed raise AND the insurance partnership pitch.

## 4. Channel strategy

### 4.1 Insurance carrier channel (highest leverage)

**Target partners (in priority order):**
1. **Chubb Commercial** — most sophisticated commercial water loss program
2. **Travelers** — biggest commercial property book in US
3. **FM Global** — loss-prevention-first culture, pioneered this category in industrial
4. **Zurich North America** — aggressive in climate tech adoption
5. **The Hartford** — large mid-market commercial footprint

**Motion:**
- Identify the Head of Commercial Property Innovation at each
- Use pilot case study as entry
- Offer carrier a co-funded pilot: they pay for sensors in 5 insured buildings, we provide data + outcomes
- Close as: carrier-subsidized deployment for their insureds (carrier pays ~40% of SaaS fee in exchange for data + loss reduction)
- Timeline: 18 months from first meeting to signed co-sell agreement

**Why this channel is a moat:**
Once Chubb has PipeWhisper in their underwriting workflow, the switching cost to a competitor is enormous. The carrier has to retrain adjusters, reintegrate software, re-sell insureds. A fast-follower has to displace a signed carrier relationship — which rarely happens.

### 4.2 Plumber network channel (flywheel)

**Motion:**
1. Partner with United Association (UA) local unions + trade schools in launch metro
2. Create "PipeWhisper Certified Plumber" program — free 2-day training + certification exam
3. Certified plumbers get:
   - Free leads routed by the system (our alerts)
   - Premium listing in our facility-side dashboard
   - A "PipeWhisper Certified" badge they can display in their trucks and marketing
   - Priority software support
4. In exchange:
   - They commit to using the PipeWhisper diagnostic app on every job
   - They label outcomes for us (fed back into the model)
   - They pay us 15% of PipeWhisper-referred job value

**Why plumbers will sign up:**
- Free leads are the rarest currency in the trade
- The certification creates legitimate marketing differentiation
- No exclusivity requirement — they keep all their existing business
- Average PipeWhisper-referred job is higher value than average walk-in (pre-qualified, non-emergency, planned)

**Network growth target:**
- Year 1: 15 plumbers (launch metro)
- Year 2: 40
- Year 3: 220 (4 metros)
- Year 4: 650 (12 metros + regional coverage)
- Year 5: 1,400 (full national coverage)

### 4.3 Direct enterprise sales (coverage for large accounts)

- Year 2 hire: VP of Sales + 2 Account Executives
- Target: 50-500 building commercial REITs, hospitality groups, university systems, healthcare systems
- 6-month sales cycle typical
- AOV: $80K-$400K first year, higher recurring

### 4.4 Municipal channel (Year 3+)

- Long sales cycles, huge contracts
- Requires dedicated municipal sales lead with local government experience
- Uses IIJA funding as the budget source
- Target: 3 lighthouse utility customers by end of Year 3

### 4.5 What we do NOT do

- **Retail** (Home Depot, Amazon) — wrong buyer, destroys brand positioning as B2B/enterprise
- **Door-to-door** — too expensive, wrong psychology
- **Paid digital ads** in Year 1 — wasted spend before the case study exists
- **Conference booth spam** — we attend AWWA and MCAA strategically but don't burn budget on 30 events

## 5. Pricing strategy evolution

### Year 1 pricing (founder-led, high touch)
- $29/sensor/month
- $499/sensor install
- 12-month minimum commit
- Friendly terms (cancel anytime after commit)
- Goal: Learn, not maximize margin

### Year 2 (scale prep)
- Tiered pricing introduced: Starter ($35/sensor), Pro ($29/sensor, 24-mo), Enterprise ($18/sensor, 36-mo, 500+)
- Install included in Pro+ tiers
- Annual prepayment discount: 10%

### Year 3+ (outcome-based options)
- Introduce "PipeWhisper Guarantee" tier: we pay the first $10K of any claim from an undetected failure. Priced at $49/sensor/month.
- Enterprise unlimited: single $400K/yr flat fee for unlimited sensors, for very large customers
- Insurance-backed plans: carrier pays 40% of SaaS in exchange for data access

## 6. Positioning and brand

**Brand position:**
*"We listen to every pipe in your building. Before it fails, we tell you — and we send the plumber."*

Key brand attributes:
- **Boring on purpose.** This is infrastructure. Not consumer tech. Design language: industrial, matte, blue-collar, reassuring.
- **Trade-respectful.** Every piece of marketing features actual plumbers, not models. We elevate the trade — we don't disrupt it.
- **Insurance-grade.** Our numbers are audited. Our predictions are backed by a guarantee. We talk like an underwriter, not a founder.

**Anti-positioning (what we're not):**
- Not a consumer gadget
- Not a "smart home" product
- Not AI replacing plumbers
- Not a Nest / Ring knockoff
- Not a dashboard for dashboard's sake

## 10. The category-creation endgame

In 5 years, we don't want to be "a predictive maintenance company." We want to be **the operating system of the plumbing trade**.

That means:
- Every commercial building in America eventually has PipeWhisper sensors (or a competitor's, but we got there first)
- Every insurance carrier has PipeWhisper data in their underwriting models
- Every plumber who wants to work on commercial jobs is PipeWhisper certified
- Every municipal water utility has a PipeWhisper dashboard on the wall
- Our brand is to plumbing what "Intel Inside" was to PCs

The category doesn't exist yet. That's the entire opportunity. The first company to define it wins forever, because categories have winners-take-most dynamics and infrastructure is sticky.

**We intend to define it.**
